\subsection{Trigger for the muon final states}

\subsubsection{Trigger choice}
To simplify the calculation of the trigger efficiency, a single di-muon trigger is used to select the muon final states. Asymmetric triggers are not considered for the analysis as they introduce spikes in the distributions of the muon transverse momentum. Such features could negatively impact the performance of the training in the machine learning selection. Therefore, the best choice would be the symmetric, dedicated \texttt{HLT\_2mu6\_bBmumuxv2\_L1LFV-MU6} trigger, which ensures smooth muon $p_T$ distributions.
The L1 topological trigger \texttt{L1LFV-MU6} identifies two muons with $p_T > 6$~GeV and angular separation $\Delta R < 1.5$.
At the HLT level, it requires a di-muon vertex with $\chi^2 < 20$ and invariant mass window $0.1 < m_{\mu\mu} < 6.5$~GeV, as well as a di-track vertex with $\chi^2 < 20$ and invariant mass window $0.1 < m_{tt} < 15$~GeV. In the final Event Filter (EF) stage, the $B^0_d \rightarrow K^{*0} \mu^+ \mu^-$ decay is reconstructed with vertex fit $\chi^2$ values less than 60 for the $B^0_d$ vertex, and less than 400 for $K^{*0}$ vertex. The candidates are also required to lie within the invariant mass windows $m_{B_d} \in [4.6, 5.9]$~GeV and $m_{K^{*0}} \in [0.7, 1.1]$~GeV.

\subsubsection{Trigger efficiency scale factor}

The trigger efficiency $\varepsilon_{\text{trig}}$, defined as the fraction of events that pass the trigger requirements relative to those selected by the same offline selections, is a crucial part of the overall efficiency. The fraction can be calculated directly using the $B^0_d$ signal MC samples. However, to account for the possible discrepancies between the simulation and the collected data, the efficiency extracted from MC has to be corrected by a scale factor (SF):

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\text{corr}}_{\text{trig}} = \varepsilon_{\text{trig}} \times \text{SF}
\end{equation}

The total scale factor can be decomposed into two parts:

\begin{equation}
\text{SF} = \text{SF}_{\text{2mu6}} \times \text{SF}_{\text{trk}},
\end{equation} 

where $\text{SF}_{\text{2mu6}}$ accounts for the scale factor related to the pure di-muon selections, and $\text{SF}_{\text{trk}}$ represents the scale factor associated with the track reconstruction in the \texttt{bBmumuxv2} topological selections.

The pure di-muon part $\text{SF}_{\text{2mu6}}$ is determined independently using reconstructed $J/\psi \to \mu^+\mu^-$ data and MC samples:

\begin{equation}
\text{SF}_{\text{2mu6}} = \dfrac{\varepsilon^{\text{data}}_{\text{2mu6}}}{\varepsilon^{\text{MC}}_{\text{2mu6}}}
\end{equation}

The pure di-muon trigger efficiency can be factorized into three distinct contributions:

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\text{2mu6}} = \varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}(1)\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}(2)\times c_{\Delta R} \times c_a
\end{equation}

$\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}$ denotes the efficiency for the \texttt{mu6} leg to identify the region of interest (RoI) of a muon. It is measured in bins of muon transverse momentum ($p_T$) and charge-signed pseudorapidity ($q\cdot\eta$). The product $\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}(1)\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}(2)$ represents the combined single muon efficiency for both muons. The $\Delta R$ correction $c_{\Delta R}$ accounts for the inefficiency arising from overlapping RoIs when the angular separation of the two muons is small. It is measured in bins of $\Delta R$ in three di-muon rapidity regions: barrel ($0 < |y^{\mu\mu}| < 1.05$), overlap ($1.05 < |y^{\mu\mu}| < 1.2$) and endcap ($|y^{\mu\mu}| > 1.2$). Finally, $c_a$ is the asymptotic correction accounting for the inefficiency due to di-muon vertexing and opposite-charge requirements. It is measured as a constant in the three rapidity regions separately. 

The three contributions are evaluated using different methods. The asymptotic correction $c_a$ is determined by counting the fraction of $J/\psi$ candidates that can pass a usual di-muon trigger relative to those that can pass a similar di-muon trigger without vertexing and opposite-charge constraints:

\begin{equation}
c_a(|y^{\mu\mu}|) = \dfrac{N_{J/\psi}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bJpsimumu}})}{N_{J/\psi}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bDimu\_novtx\_noos}})}
\end{equation}

The $J/\psi$ signal yields $N_{J/\psi}$ are obtained by fitting the di-muon invariant mass distribution in the range $2.7 < m_{\mu\mu} < 3.5$~GeV. The asymptotic correction reaches saturation rapidly as a function of $\Delta R$, and is therefore extracted by fitting a constant value in the region $\Delta R > 0.12$, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:fit_c_a}.

The $\Delta R$ correction is derived using di-muon tag-and-probe method. In this approach, the tag muon is required to match to the HLT muon of the tag trigger \texttt{HLT\_mu10\_bJpsi\_TrkPEB} within $\Delta R < 0.01$,  
and also to match to the corresponding L1 RoI using muon parameters extrapolated to the trigger pivot. The L1 matching criterion is defined as $\Delta R < \text{L1Cut}(p_T)$, where
 the $p_T$-dependent cut is given by
\begin{equation}
\text{L1Cut}(p_T) = \left\{
\begin{aligned}
-0.01p_T + 0.18 &, \text{ if } p_T < 10 \text{ GeV} \\
0.08 &, \text{ otherwise}
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}

Define the ratio $\rho_{\Delta R}$ as:

\begin{equation}
\rho_{\Delta R}(\Delta R, |y^{\mu\mu}|) = \dfrac{N_{J/\psi}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_mu10\_bJpsi\_TrkPEB} \&\& \texttt{HLT\_2mu10\_bJpsimumu}})}{N_{J/\psi}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_mu10\_bJpsi\_TrkPEB}})},
\end{equation}

where the two muons are required to have $p_T > 12 \text{ GeV}$ to ensure that the \texttt{mu10}  efficiency is in the plateau region to get rid of single muon effect. The $\rho_{\Delta R}$ ratio is fitted as a function of $\Delta R$ using an error function with a turn-on parameter $\sigma$ and normalization factor $N$ in the three rapidity regions:

\begin{equation}
f(\Delta R;N,\sigma) = N\text{Erf}(\Delta R/\sigma)
\end{equation}

The fits are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:fit_rho_dr}. The true correction $c_{\Delta R}$ is obtained by removing the normalisation $N$, thereby isolating the dependence on $\Delta R$.

The same tag-and-probe muon pairs used to extract $c_{\Delta R}$ are also employed to determine the single muon trigger efficiency. The single efficiency $\varepsilon_{\text{muX}}$ of the single muon trigger with $p_T$ threshold $X$~GeV is obtained by first computing the ratio:

\begin{equation}
  \rho_{J/ \psi}(p^{\text{probe}}_T, q\cdot \eta^{\text{probe}}, \Delta R, |y^{\mu\mu}|)
  = \dfrac{N_{J/ \psi}(\text{\texttt{muX} tagged \&\& \texttt{2muX} di-muon trigger})}{N_{J/ \psi}(\text{\texttt{muX} tagged})}
\label{eq:rho_jpsi}
\end{equation}

This ratio is then weighted by the the inverse of the overall di-muon correction to isolate the single muon efficiency:

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\text{muX}}(p_T, q\cdot\eta) = \dfrac{\rho_{J/ \psi}(p_T, q\cdot\eta, \Delta R, |y^{\mu\mu}|)}{c_{\Delta R}(\Delta R, |y^{\mu\mu}|)\times c_a(|y^{\mu\mu}|)}
\end{equation}

However, in 2018, un-prescaled and symmetric \texttt{2mu6} di-muon triggers without additional topological selections were not available to select the events in the numerator of formula \ref{eq:rho_jpsi}. As a result, the $\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}$ efficiency could only be extracted using the asymmetric
\texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bJpsimumu} trigger. Furthermore, the available tag trigger with the highest $p_T$ threshold was \texttt{HLT\_mu10\_bJpsi\_TrkPEB}. To ensure reliable extraction of $\varepsilon_{\text{mu6}}$, all tag muons are required to have $p_T > 11$~GeV. Moreover, for events with probe muon $p_T < 11$~GeV and rejected by \texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bJpsimumu}, only those where the probe muon is rejected by \texttt{HLT\_mu6\_nomucomb} are retained. This helps remove cases where the rejection of \texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bJpsimumu} is due to the tag muon failing the \texttt{mu11} leg while the probe muon passes the \texttt{mu6} leg. Without this cut, the single efficiency would be significantly underestimated. Fig.~\ref{fig:Run1_singlemueffmap} shows the 2D maps of \texttt{mu6} efficiency as a function of  $p_T$ and $q \cdot \eta$. Significant drops in efficiency can be observed near $|\eta|\approx 1.0$ (barrel-endcap transition region) and $|\eta| \approx 0$ (central crack).

The track-associated scale factor $\text{SF}_{\text{trk}}$ is determined using high-$q^2$ $B^0_d \to K^{*0}J/\psi(\mu^+\mu^-)$ and $B^0_d \to K^{*0}\mu^+\mu^-$ data and MC samples:

\begin{equation}
\text{SF}_{\text{trk}} = \dfrac{\varepsilon^{\text{data}}_{\text{trk}}}{\varepsilon^{\text{MC}}_{\text{trk}}},
\end{equation}

where the track selection efficiency $\varepsilon_{\text{trk}}$ is obtained from the fraction of $B^0_d$ candidates that pass both the \texttt{HLT\_2mu6\_bBmumuxv2\_L1LFV-MU6} and \texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bDimu} triggers among those required to pass only \texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bDimu}, as a function of di-track $p_T$:

\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\text{trk}}(p_T^{tt}) = \dfrac{N_{B^0_d}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_2mu6\_bBmumuxv2\_L1LFV-MU6} \&\& \texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bDimu}})}{N_{B^0_d}(\text{\texttt{HLT\_mu11\_mu6\_bDimu} })}
\end{equation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:trk_eff} shows the track selection efficiencies in data18 and MC16e. The efficiency has a smaller value at low di-track $p_T$ but approaches unity as $p_T^{tt}$ increases.
